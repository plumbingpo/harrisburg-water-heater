If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Harrisburg, PA, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless heaters skip the sediment bed but have a scale problem of their own. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually causing shutdowns. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the water filter, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
