How Steelton Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
