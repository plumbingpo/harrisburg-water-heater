Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

A little rust-colored water or a relief valve that drips now and then can be the first hint of a tank that is close to the end. If you notice any of these signs, contact Harrisburg Water Heater at (717) 256-8635 for a prompt inspection.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
